# Classroom pilot: Lectures 02 to 05

A protocol to find out, with real data, whether the novice-first redesign helps. Until it has been carried out, every
claim about learning is **NOT VERIFIED — requires classroom data**.

## Questions

1. Can students who start from zero reach the outcomes of Lectures 02-05 (in `roadmaps.md`)?
2. Which concepts remain unclear after the lecture (the misconceptions of `rubrics.md`)?
3. Do students perceive clear objectives, adequate prerequisites and clear connections (pulse survey)?

## Instruments

| When | Instrument | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start of Lecture 02 | **Pre-check**: the two exit-ticket questions of Lecture 02 and question A04.1, unannounced and not marked | 5 min |
| End of each of Lectures 02-05 | **Exit ticket** of the lecture (`exit-tickets.md`) | 3 min |
| End of each of Lectures 02-05 | **Pulse survey** (`pulse-survey.md`) | 2 min |
| End of each lecture, optional | **Perceived effort**: "How much mental effort did today's lecture require?" 1 (very little) to 9 (very much) | 1 min |
| Start of Lecture 06 | **Post-check**: the same questions as the pre-check, plus A05.1 and A05.2 | 8 min |

## Scoring

Score each answer with the rubric of `rubrics.md`: **2** right with reasoning, **1** partly right, **0** wrong or blank.
Record also whether the answer shows the misconception named in the rubric. Two people should score a sample of 20
answers independently and compare; discuss disagreements and adjust the reading of the rubric before scoring the rest.

## Recording, privacy and consent

- Collect papers without names. If pre- and post-checks must be paired, students write a pseudonym of their choice on
  both; the key is never collected.
- Record only counts per question: number of 0, 1, 2 and of each misconception; for the survey, the counts of each value.
- Tell the class beforehand what is collected, why, and that taking part has no effect on marks. Anyone may hand in a blank sheet.
- Store the counts, not the papers; destroy the papers once counted. Check with the university whether the study
  needs ethics approval or a formal information notice (GDPR) before publishing any result outside the course.

## Analysis

- For each item: the proportion of 2, 1, 0 and of the main misconception; for the pre/post questions, the change in
  the proportion of correct answers.
- For the survey: the median and the distribution of each statement, per lecture.
- Read the free-text answers and group them by topic.

**Caveats.** One class, one year, no control group: a change between pre- and post-check cannot be attributed to the
redesign alone (students also study, attend the laboratory and use other material). Small groups make proportions
unstable: report counts, not percentages, below 30 answers. No claim of statistical significance should be made from this pilot.

## Decision rubric: what to change

| Observation | Threshold (suggested) | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An exit-ticket item is answered right (2) by fewer than half | < 50 % | Revisit the concept at the start of the next lecture with a new worked example; check that the slide that teaches it has its checkpoint |
| One misconception appears in more than a quarter of the answers | > 25 % | Add a slide or a note that names the misconception and shows why it fails; add it to the checkpoint |
| Address and value are still confused at the post-check (A02 checkpoint 2, deck question on `la`/`lw`) | > 25 % wrong | Add a second trace exercise to Lecture 04 or 05 using an array; use the two-arrow diagram again |
| Pulse statement 1 (objectives) has a median of 3 or less | ≤ 3 | Read the roadmap aloud at the start and come back to it at the end |
| Pulse statement 2 (prerequisites) has a median of 3 or less | ≤ 3 | Point to *Starting from Zero* and the glossary before the lecture; check the first use of the terms of the lecture |
| Perceived effort above 7 for most students | > 7 | Split the lecture: move one USE NOW topic to the code deck or the next lecture |

Record every decision and its reason in `docs/audit`, so that the next iteration can compare.
