# A lecture of 60 to 90 minutes: facilitation template

An adaptable plan that follows the spiral of the course (play, observe, formalise, return). Times are for a lecture
of 90 minutes; for 60 minutes shorten the explanation and the practice, never the prediction and the closing.

| Phase | Minutes | What happens | Source in the material |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1. Orientation | 5 | Read the roadmap aloud: what today builds on, the two or three outcomes, the next step. Answer, in two minutes, the exit ticket of the previous lecture. | header of the notes; `roadmaps.md` |
| 2. Retrieval | 5 | One recall question from an earlier lecture: the bridge of today, if there is one, or a question of the previous exit ticket that many got wrong. | `bridges.json`; last exit ticket |
| 3. Live demo | 10 | Run the warm-up program on PRG32-QT. Do not explain yet. | first listing of the code deck |
| 4. Prediction | 5 | Before running a variant: everyone writes what will happen, then compares with a neighbour. Collect two or three predictions aloud, including a wrong one. | a checkpoint slide, or a question of the deck |
| 5. Explanation | 25 | The formal part: one concept at a time, each closed by a short check (a show of hands, a one-line answer). | lecture deck, slides with UNDERSTAND NOW |
| 6. Practice | 20 | A trace by hand or a change to the program, in pairs. Walk around; listen for the misconception of the rubric. | exercises; `rubrics.md` |
| 7. Retrieval | 5 | Back to the prediction of phase 4: who was right, and why? | |
| 8. Closing | 10 | Read the closing thread; the exit ticket, alone, on paper, three minutes; announce the next step. | last slide notes; `exit-tickets.md` |

## Rules that make it work

- **Predict before running, always.** A wrong prediction is the most useful event of the lecture; make it safe to be wrong.
- **One concept at a time.** If a slide raises a second question, write it on the board with the lecture that answers it.
- **Name the status.** Say whether something is to understand now, to use now or to understand later (see the guide).
- **Pause where the notes say so.** Each checkpoint has a pause of two or three minutes and a likely wrong answer.
- **Recover the student who is stuck.** In a laboratory step: pair them with a neighbour whose program runs, or give them
  the reference program of the step (for Verm1, the file of the previous part) and ask them to find the one line that differs.

## After the lecture

Read the exit tickets the same day. Count, for each question, *right*, *partly right*, *wrong*, and note the most
common wrong answer. Start the next lecture with it (phase 1). The pilot protocol describes how to record these counts.
